# Storyline

Write the outline as one line per slide before you touch layouts. Each line is the slide's headline. If the headlines read top to bottom tell the whole story, the deck works.

## Default structure

1. **Cover.** Project or topic, audience organisation, month and year.
2. **Summary.** The one sentence the audience must remember, plus three to five numbers that back it.
3. **Sections**, each opened by a divider numbered `01_`, `02_`, `03_`. Typical sections for an update: progress (what happened), the core topic (how it works), cases (what it looks like in practice).
4. **Next steps.** Roadmap or phases, then what you need from the audience.
5. **Close.** One line that restates the summary, then questions.

Put progress before explanation when the audience is waiting to hear what happened (a project update). Put explanation first when the topic is new to them (a proposal).

## Headlines

- A headline is a sentence that states the point: "7 of 22 agencies visited so far", not "Site visit status".
- One line at 56 px holds about 55 characters on 1728 px of width. Two lines is the limit.
- Keep one grammar through the deck: all statements, or all short noun phrases for structural slides. Do not mix questions in.
- No drama constructions ("It's not X, it's Y"), no rhetorical questions, no colons used as a reveal.

## Eyebrows and numbering

- The eyebrow above each headline names the section and the slide's role: `02_ Data fusion · Sources`.
- Section numbers are two digits and an underscore. Steps in a flow are numbered 1, 2, 3.
- When you insert a section, renumber every later divider, eyebrow and page number.

## Slide budget

- About one minute per content slide; dividers and the cover take seconds.
- A 20 minute slot: 12 to 18 slides. A 10 minute slot: 7 to 10.
- When the source has more slides than the budget, merge first (see `simplify.md`), then move the rest to an appendix the presenter can open if asked.

## Endings by purpose

| Purpose | Last content slide |
|---|---|
| Inform | Next steps with dates or phases |
| Decide | The decisions needed, numbered, each with a one-line reason |
| Persuade | The proposal and the first concrete step |
