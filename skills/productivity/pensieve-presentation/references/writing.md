# Writing for slides

## Language

- **Body text in the audience's language.** Headlines, leads, cells, notes.
- **Technical terms stay in English** when that is how practitioners say them. Translating them makes the deck harder to read for the people who use the terms daily.
- **Structural words can be English** when the source or the brand uses them (`USE CASE`, `PROJECT UPDATE`, `Next steps`). Pick one and keep it.
- **Institutions by their official abbreviation**, written exactly as the institution writes it (TNI AL, Bakamla RI, BMKG). In a deck the abbreviation alone is fine after the first mention.

### English terms to keep (extend per project)

Data fusion, Common Operational Picture (COP), data source, dashboard, real-time, track, confidence level, confidence scoring, correlation, deduplication, early warning, situational awareness, anomaly detection, risk analysis, use case, stakeholder, trigger, outcome, roadmap, phase, next steps, watch officer, data sharing, data governance, secure communication, API, data lake, geospatial database, role-based access control, audit logging, feed, imagery, OSINT.

Mixing is normal and natural: "Fusion Engine mencocokkan keempatnya, lalu memberi confidence level." Inflect English words the way speakers do ("di-update", "di-overlay", "track-nya").

## Tone

- **Natural, the way a capable presenter talks**, not the way a report reads. Short sentences. Everyday words where they read better ("supaya", "dicek", "jadi").
- **Formal audiences:** keep the voice impersonal ("Sistem ini menyatukan…", "Data diverifikasi…"), no "kami" on slides; "kami" is fine in speaker notes.
- **No marketing adjectives, no exclamation marks, no emoji.**
- **Say who does what.** "Watch officer memverifikasi dengan data intelijen" beats "Dilakukan verifikasi".

### Passive voice in Indonesian

Formal Indonesian uses the di- passive naturally and widely. Do not force active voice where a native speaker would write "Data diverifikasi" or "Peringatan dikirim ke pos terdekat". The rule from the copy gate still applies to English text, and to Indonesian sentences that hide an actor the reader needs to know.

## Numbers and placeholders

- Numbers only from the source or the person. Otherwise `[__]`.
- Dates as the audience writes them ("8 Oktober 2026", "October 8, 2026").
- Units and thousands separators in the audience's convention (1.250 in Indonesian, 1,250 in English).

## Length per element (1920 px slide)

| Element | Size | Rough limit |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 56 px | 55 characters per line, 2 lines |
| Lead | 28 px | 2 lines |
| Table cell, step, finding | 24 px | 1 to 2 lines |
| Card or matrix cell | 26 px | 20 words |
| Notes key message | n/a | One sentence |
